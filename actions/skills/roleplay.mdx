---
title: "Role Play Skills"
---

A role play skill turns your Double into a character for a practice conversation — a hiring manager, a sceptical buyer, an investor, a coach, or a new colleague. The visitor practises, your Double stays in character, and you get a scored report at the end.

**Where to find it:** **Library → Skills → Role play**, or open a Double → **Configure → Skills**.

## Choose a scenario

| Scenario | Your Double plays | You provide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Job Interview** | A hiring manager | The role being interviewed for; company or industry |
| **Sales Call** | A buyer on the other side of the call | What the visitor is selling; objections to raise |
| **Investor Pitch** | An investor | Stage or round; what the company does |
| **Coaching** | A coach | What the visitor wants to work on; coaching approach |
| **Onboarding** | A new colleague being onboarded | What they are being onboarded to; what new joiners usually get wrong |
| **Custom** | A role you describe | The situation; what the visitor is practising; how the Double should behave |

Each scenario comes with its own behaviour rules and a scoring rubric. For example, a sales call is scored on discovery, listening, objection handling, and closing on a next step.

## Configure the session

### Role

The **Role** field label changes with the scenario — for example, *"Who should the Double interview the user as?"*. Write who your Double is playing, including tone and seniority if it matters.

### Difficulty

| Level | How your Double behaves |
| --- | --- |
| **Easy** | Warm, with hints |
| **Medium** | Neutral and strict |
| **Hard** | Pushes back |

Difficulty also affects scoring: the same answer earns more credit at a harder level.

### Voice and persona

Choose:

- **Double's own persona** — the role play uses your Double's personality.
- **Custom role persona** — set a **Persona style** (Neutral, Friendly, Challenging, Supportive, Strict) and optional **Additional persona directions** (up to 500 characters) for this role only.

This controls the character, not the audio voice. Voice calls use the Double's normal voice.

### Suggested questions

Add the questions you want covered, reorder them, or click **Use scenario defaults**.

> Double can adapt these to the conversation.

The Double asks them in order but can follow up naturally. Adding or removing suggested questions does not change the session length setting.

### Anything else Double should know

Add background, context, or focus areas — up to 2,000 characters. For example:

> Focus on product sense and prioritisation. The company sells to mid-market finance teams.

### When the session ends

- **After a set number of questions** — 1 to 30
- **After a time limit** — 1 to 120 minutes
- **When Double has covered everything**

### During the session

- **Give feedback at the end**
- **Allow pause and resume**
- **Allow restart**
- **Allow repeating a question**

### Start phrases

Add optional exact phrases for a guaranteed start, such as `practise for my interview`. Without them, Double decides when the skill applies.

### Conversational pacing

Control how often Double suggests the skill:

- **Cooldown turns** — how many turns to wait before suggesting it again
- **Max suggested attempts** — how many times it may promote the skill
- **Drop or defer immediately if visitor expresses hesitation or pushback**

## Reference files and hand-offs

- **Reference knowledge** — files Double reads during the session, such as a job description or rubric. Visitors never receive them.
- **Files Double hands over** — shareable files the participant can receive when they ask, or automatically at the end.

## What the visitor experiences

1. The visitor says something that matches the skill, or Double offers it.
2. Your Double opens in character with a greeting and the first question.
3. Each reply is one turn in character — usually one question at a time.
4. The session ends at the configured limit, or when the visitor says **stop**, **end**, **exit**, **quit**, **cancel**, or **finish**.
5. The Double closes in character — often *"That's everything from me. Thanks for your time."*

Visitors do **not** see a scorecard or rubric. The report is for you.

Role plays work in chat and voice. On voice, the conversation is transcribed and spoken as usual.

## What you see afterwards

Open the skill's **Results** tab:

| Card | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Sessions** | Total sessions |
| **Completed** | Sessions that reached an end condition |
| **Average score** | Mean overall score |

The table lists each session with its date, number of questions, length, score, and status.

Each session stores:

- The full transcript
- The exact configuration used at the time
- An overall score out of 100
- Four rubric scores for the scenario
- **Key strengths** with a quoted moment from the transcript
- **Opportunities for improvement** with a concrete alternative

Scoring is graded relative to the difficulty you chose. Scores above 85 are reserved for genuinely exceptional sessions.

Use **Test** to run a real session yourself before sharing the Double.

## Tips

- Give the role enough detail to be believable — seniority, company stage, and what they care about.
- Use **Hard** for realistic pressure and **Easy** when someone is learning the basics.
- Add reference files when the scenario depends on a specific document.
- Keep suggested questions to the few things that must be covered; the Double adapts the rest.
- Check a few transcripts and rubric notes to see whether the difficulty matches your audience.

## What to know

- A role play needs at least one Double assigned.
- Only the first assigned Double is used for the persona and voice.
- Feedback is generated when the session ends; a failed generation leaves the session without a report.
- Sessions are recorded under your account, so all visitors appear in the same Results list without names.
- Time limits are checked when the visitor replies, so an idle session can run past the limit.

## FAQ

**Why didn't the role play start?** Check that the skill is **Active**, assigned to the Double, and that the visitor's message matches a start phrase or clearly asks to practise.

**Can the visitor pause or restart?** Those controls are part of your session settings, but visitors mainly interact by chatting. They can always end the session by asking to stop.

**Why is the score low?** Scores reflect the selected difficulty and the scenario rubric. Review the strengths and improvement notes for specifics.

**Does it work on voice calls?** Yes. The session continues on voice with speech-to-text and spoken replies.

**Can I see who practised?** Results are pooled under your account and do not identify individual visitors.

**How do I stop a session early?** The visitor can say stop, end, exit, quit, cancel, or finish. You can also end an owner test session with **End Session**.
